As a simulation technologist in Liberia for the past 4 years, my project is a simple patient triage system designed to help healthcare workers quickly assess patients and determine the urgency of medical attention. I have seen patients dying, and they had to rush them to the ER while they walked through the triage and were sent to the OPD. The system allows clinicians to enter basic patient information and vital signs such as heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation. Based on these inputs, the program categorizes patients into different priority levels (High, Medium, and Low), helping healthcare providers make faster and more informed decisions.
I chose this project because of my interest in healthcare and my desire to use technology to improve health services and systems in Liberia, particularly in rural areas where medical resources are often limited. The project also aligns with my long-term goal of developing digital health solutions that can support healthcare workers and improve patient outcomes.
Developing this project was both challenging and rewarding. As a beginner programmer, I learned how to organize code, use functions, work with conditional statements, and handle user input effectively. One of the most interesting parts was designing the logic to evaluate vital signs and determine patient priority levels.
Throughout the process, I improved my problem-solving skills and gained an understanding of how software can be applied to real-world healthcare challenges. This project has strengthened my confidence in programming and motivated me to continue learning Python and building more advanced health technology solutions in the future.
